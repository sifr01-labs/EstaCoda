## Measured behavior versus implementation inference

### 1. Cancellation between preparation and publication

**Measured:** The existing test creates a running attempt with a lease, prepares a fenced result, requests attempt cancellation, then attempts publication inside `store.atomicWrite`. It observes:

- Error code **`result-fence-lost`**.
- No result metadata remains.
- The prepared content body is absent after `discardPrepared`.

Evidence: `src/tasks/task-result-service.test.ts:318-371`.

This is a deterministic ordering test, not a simultaneous multi-process race test. It does not explicitly assert absence of a `result-recorded` event in this cancellation case.

**Implementation inference:** The same fence predicate rejects a missing lease, different owner, different fencing token, expired lease (`expiresAt <= now`), or any defined `cancellationRequestedAt` (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:684-698`). Those individual variants were not separately measured by this executed cancellation test.

### 2. Publication rollback and body cleanup

**Measured:**

- Event persistence failure rolls back result metadata and removes its body (`src/tasks/task-result-service.test.ts:278-291`).
- A later failure in a two-result publication batch leaves no result metadata, no `result-recorded` events, and neither content body after discard (`src/tasks/task-result-service.test.ts:293-316`).
- Recovery removes abandoned prepared bodies and preserves bodies whose metadata committed before marker cleanup (`src/tasks/task-result-service.test.ts:373-402`).

**Implementation inference:** Preparation writes bodies while withholding metadata. Publication revalidates the batch, inserts metadata and journal events, and expects the caller to perform Attempt/Step/Task settlement within the same transaction (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:181-245`). `record` provides its own atomic metadata publication and invokes finalization or discard (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:169-179`).

These tests support transactional publication and compensating filesystem cleanup; they do not establish an atomic transaction spanning SQLite and the filesystem.

## Accept/reject gates

### Preparation

The service validates:

- Result kind, per-result size, summary/display-summary bounds, MIME length, and expiry syntax (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:304-336`).
- Task existence and Attempt/Task, Step/Task, and Attempt/Step relationships (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:338-359`).
- Lease validity **only if `expectedLease` is present** (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:347-352`).
- Accepted-result kind compatibility with the Step policy (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:360-361`).
- Unique result IDs/handles within a prepared batch, body writes, and integrity verification (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:181-209`).

Disposition defaults to **`accepted`** (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:360`).

### Publication

`publishPrepared` revalidates before inserting any batch metadata (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:218-221`). Revalidation checks identity relationships and the optional lease again (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:393-414`), then applies Step policy and aggregate byte budgets, separately by disposition (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:416-437`).

**Important limitation:** These validation paths check Task existence, not Task status; likewise, they do not independently reject terminal Step/Attempt statuses. The cancellation gate is the optional lease predicate, not a general lifecycle-status predicate (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:338-361`, `src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:393-418`, `src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:684-698`).

### Cleanup and recovery

- `finalizePrepared` removes preparation markers after commit (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:250-255`).
- `discardPrepared` removes uncommitted bodies but preserves a body whose matching metadata committed (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:258-264`, `src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:470-482`).
- Recovery reconciles pending markers against stored result metadata (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:267-302`).

Consequently, cancellation after a successful publication is not, by itself, a result-revocation mechanism.

## Publication, inspection, and delivery

Read access is distinct from acceptance:

- `preview` checks result/task association, availability, retention, session authorization, and content integrity (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:488-496`).
- `readText` checks authorization, association, availability, expiry, textual readability, integrity, and UTF-8 validity (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:525-560`).
- Neither read gate requires accepted disposition or an uncancelled Task. Diagnostic output can remain available for authorized inspection.

The executed suite passed tests for diagnostic inspection, authorization, expiry, tampering, pruning, and result-tool read/export behavior. This measures the named existing cases, not every possible cancellation/delivery interleaving.

Primary-answer and delivery accounting apply additional selection rules:

- Primary synthesis selection requires an **available, accepted** result for the synthesis Step (`src/tasks/task-primary-result.ts:27-33`).
- Research completion requires completed workers with accepted available results (`src/tasks/task-primary-result.ts:5-20`).
- A delivered report path requires a completed report-delivery Step with an accepted available result (`src/tasks/task-primary-result.ts:51-57`).

These helpers do not independently revoke results upon Task cancellation. Result readability, primary-answer selection, and successful delivery accounting are therefore separate properties.

## Scheduler boundary and concrete composition risk

Limited observed scheduler call-site evidence shows:

- Lease expectations supplied at `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1434` and `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1744`.
- Prepared publication at `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1478` and `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1760`.
- Finalization/discard calls at `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1532-1534` and `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1800-1802`.

**Evidence limitation:** Bounded reads of the surrounding scheduler source returned truncated content. These references establish call-site presence only; they do not independently prove every scheduler settlement guard or transaction ordering. That proof belongs with the scheduler investigator.

### Concrete risk: an omitted fence bypasses cancellation rejection

The API permits an attempt-associated result without `expectedLease`. Such a result defaults to accepted; neither preparation nor publication independently rejects cancelled/terminal lifecycle status.

Thus, a caller that omits the fence can bypass this service’s cancellation rejection gate. This is an implementation-level boundary risk, **not a demonstrated production scheduler failure**: the observed scheduler call sites supply fences.

### Testable composition argument

The boundary composes if the scheduler guarantees that:

1. Every attempt-derived accepted publication carries the current lease owner/token.
2. Cancellation is persisted in the lease state that publication re-reads.
3. Fence validation, result/event insertion, and lifecycle settlement occur in the same effective transaction.
4. Rejected publication discards prepared content and cannot cause delivery.

The executed service cancellation test directly supports the service half of this argument for **cancellation before publication**.

Recommended existing-suite coverage to verify or add later—not implemented here:

- Fence omission on a cancelled attempt, explicitly documenting whether rejection is required.
- Owner/token replacement and expiry between preparation and publication.
- Task/Step cancellation without a lease cancellation marker.
- End-to-end cancellation immediately before settlement, asserting no accepted result, result-recorded event, or delivery receipt.
- Cancellation after commit, explicitly documenting whether already accepted results remain inspectable/deliverable.
