All 81 selected tests passed. Scheduler publication is cancellation-fenced, but direct service calls can omit fencing; true late success after operator cancellation remains untested.

# Release audit: cancellation and late-result rejection

## Release assessment

**Conditional confidence in the inspected scheduler-to-result-service path; insufficient evidence for an unconditional release claim that all late results are rejected.**

The scheduler supplies a lease fence, revalidates durable state inside its settlement transaction, publishes results through that transaction, and completes the attempt and step before releasing the fenced lease. The result service independently rechecks a supplied fence at publication. These source-level contracts compose coherently, and existing tests measured cancellation between preparation and publication, cooperative cancellation, cancellation before executor invocation, and rollback of result/completion writes under injected failures.

Two qualifications materially limit release assurance:

1. **The service’s fence is optional.** It is not a standalone terminal-state firewall. An alternate caller omitting `expectedLease` bypasses the inspected cancellation/ownership/expiry checks, while disposition defaults to `accepted`.
2. **The scheduler’s named “rejects late success” operator-cancellation test returns a cancelled outcome, not late success.** Its passing result does not prove rejection of an executor that ignores operator cancellation and later submits successful output.

A release requiring demonstrated protection against that adversarial interleaving should remain gated on the regression checks below. No failing test or demonstrated production exploit was reported.

## 3. State, ownership, and late-result comparison

| Concern | Scheduler behavior — S | Result-service behavior — R | Audit implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Durable cancellation | Persists cancellation before aborting local execution. Active leased attempts may await settlement. | Checks a lease cancellation marker when a fence is supplied; does not itself perform task cancellation. | Durable cancellation and local executor termination are separate events. |
| Admission | Revalidates running task, ready step, queued attempt, dispatch ownership, capacity, and lease acquisition transactionally. | Validates task/attempt/step relationships during preparation/publication. | Service relationship checks do not replace scheduler admission checks. |
| Ownership | Requires current owner ID and fencing token. | Requires matching owner/token only with `expectedLease`. | Consistency depends on callers supplying the fence. |
| Expiry | Rejects expiry at or before scheduler time, including cancellation settlement. | Rejects expiry at or before service time when fenced. | A cancelled task can coexist with a late attempt accounted as lease-lost rather than successfully settled as cancelled. |
| Terminal state | Checks terminal task before success and again through transactional lease validation; requires a running step before publication. | No explicit task/step/attempt terminal-state rejection observed in inspected validation. | Terminal-state protection is principally a scheduler responsibility. |
| Cancellation during preparation | Revalidates inside the success-settlement transaction. | Rechecks supplied fence at publication, not only preparation. | Prepared content is not authorization to publish later. |
| Accepted success | Publishes results and completes attempt/step using one transactional store, then finalizes content. | Publishes metadata/events through supplied store; caller must settle state in the same transaction. | The transaction boundary is a key compositional invariant. |
| Operator-cancelled late success | Source routes terminal/cancellation-marked settlement into cancellation before success publication. | Fenced publication rejects cancellation marker. | Source-supported, but true late-success payload after operator cancellation lacks direct end-to-end measurement. |
| Deadline-late success | Existing test retains late output as diagnostic while failing execution. | Diagnostic output can be retained/read without being accepted primary output. | “Reject late success” is not equivalent to deleting every late body. |
| Read/export | Acceptance and overall task completion are separate from body availability. | Authorized retained available output can be read/exported without requiring a noncancelled task or accepted disposition. | Readability/exportability is not proof of successful completion. |
| Plan/attempt generation | Owner/token fencing observed; token monotonicity and separate generation guarantees not established. | No explicit latest-attempt or active-plan generation gate observed. | Do not equate token equality with proven newest-plan membership. |

### 3.1 Cancellation state transitions

`TaskScheduler.cancelTask()` invokes durable cancellation before aborting matching local executions (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:424–437`, S).

Inside an atomic write, the helper:

- Leaves already-terminal tasks unchanged.
- Cancels active-plan steps in pending/ready/input-waiting/approval-waiting states.
- Immediately cancels queued attempts and unleased input/approval-waiting attempts.
- Requests cancellation on other active attempts.
- Marks the task cancelled and records its timestamp and event.

References: `src/tasks/task-operator-service.ts:791–806`, `src/tasks/task-operator-service.ts:816–849` (S).

Thus a task becoming durably cancelled does **not** imply every leased attempt and running step has already terminalized.

Before executor invocation, the scheduler honors an already-aborted signal; cancellation-related executor exceptions become cancelled settlements (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1139–1172`). At settlement, terminal task state, a lease cancellation marker, or an explicit cancelled outcome routes to cancellation rather than success (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1349–1354`).

Cancellation settlement validates the current lease while allowing its cancellation marker, marks attempt/step cancellation, releases the exact lease, and appends events without publishing results (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1687–1714`).

### 3.2 Ownership and expiry remain enforced during cancellation

The scheduler’s lease assertion checks task/attempt/lease existence, owner, token, expiry, and cancellation permission; ordinary settlement additionally rejects terminal task state (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1956–1978`, S).

Allowing cancellation does not waive ownership or expiry. If fenced cancellation settlement also loses the lease, the run records lease loss rather than claiming successful cancellation settlement (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1190–1202`).

Heartbeat uses owner/token fencing; rejected renewal produces lease loss, while a returned cancellation marker aborts locally (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:440–454`).

The result service uses the corresponding owner/token/expiry/cancellation checks when `expectedLease` is present, rejecting violations as `result-fence-lost` (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:684–698`, R).

Neither investigation established concrete token-allocation monotonicity or a separate generation counter.

## 4. Why the inspected boundaries compose

### Source-derived invariant

**Accepted scheduler success requires publication and attempt/step completion to share the same transaction and remain conditioned on the same current attempt lease.**

The scheduler:

1. Validates result acceptance and execution limits (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1405–1414`, S).
2. Supplies task/step/attempt identity and expected owner/token to `prepare()` (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1421–1438`, S).
3. Opens its settlement transaction, rechecks the lease, and requires the step still be running (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1462–1474`, S).
4. Calls `publishPrepared(preparedResults, store)` with that transactional store (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1476–1484`, S).
5. Records usage, completion, task state, fenced lease release, and events within the transaction (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1485–1530`, S).
6. Finalizes prepared content after success, or discards it on error (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1532–1546`, S).

The result-service half matches that protocol:

- Preparation validates identities and a supplied lease (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:338–361`, R).
- Publication revalidates against the store supplied to publication (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:393–417`, R).
- Validation precedes result metadata and journal insertion (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:217–247`, R).
- `result-fence-lost` is translated by the scheduler into lease loss rather than accepted completion (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1436–1438`, `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1479–1483`, S).

This is a **specific, testable composition argument grounded in both scopes**, not proof of all concurrent executions or underlying database isolation.

### Prepared content versus published output

`record()` wraps publication in an atomic write and finalizes or discards appropriately (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:166–176`). Preparation writes/verifies bodies while result metadata remains absent (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:179–210`). Cleanup preserves bodies whose metadata actually committed (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:258–264`, `src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:469–481`, R).

This distinguishes a staged file from a published result. It does not establish crash-proof cleanup under every interruption.

## 6. Concrete cross-subsystem risks

### Risk A: alternate publication bypasses scheduler protection

**Source-derived boundary risk, not an executed failure or demonstrated exploit.**

`expectedLease` is optional (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:33–46`). Lease validation is conditional during preparation and publication (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:345–351`, `src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:403–407`). Inspected service validation does not independently reject terminal task/step/attempt state (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:393–437`), and disposition defaults to accepted (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:360–372`).

The inspected SQLite `recordResult()` supplies transaction/profile/task-ownership and content constraints, but no explicit terminal-state or lease check in that method (`src/tasks/sqlite-task-store.ts:1001–1037`, R).

By contrast, scheduler protection explicitly relies on the expected lease and guarded transaction (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1421–1438`, `src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1462–1513`, S).

**Concrete failure hypothesis:** a new or alternate caller records stale output after cancellation without `expectedLease`; the inspected service path does not supply the scheduler’s missing cancellation/terminal protection. The full set of production callers was not audited, so this is not a claim that such a caller currently exists.

### Risk B: passing test name overstates late-success coverage

The operator-cancellation test at `src/tasks/task-scheduler.test.ts:841–859` resolves cancellation cooperatively. Meanwhile, R directly measured service rejection only after preparation with a supplied fence (`src/tasks/task-result-service.test.ts:318–371`).

Together these leave the actual end-to-end interleaving—operator cancellation followed by an abort-ignoring executor’s successful payload—**source-supported but not directly measured**.

### Risk C: readable output mistaken for accepted completion

Authorized reads require available, retained, integrity-valid output, but do not independently require an accepted disposition or noncancelled task (`src/tasks/task-result-service.ts:528–565`). Export uses that read boundary and records disposition (`src/tools/task-result-tools.ts:107–179`, R).

Primary selection separately requires an available accepted result tied to the active revision’s synthesis step; completion accounting also requires accepted output and completed worker steps (`src/tasks/task-primary-result.ts:5–38`, R). Scheduler task completion can remain partial despite completed steps when research completion or requested file output is pending (`src/tasks/task-scheduler.ts:1497–1508`, S).

Consequently, retained diagnostic or historical output can remain inspectable without constituting successful task completion. Downstream consumers must preserve that distinction.

## 7. Untested interleavings and material limitations

The evidence does **not** establish exhaustive behavior for:

- An executor ignoring **operator** cancellation and returning successful results afterward.
- Cancellation after the initial settlement check but before/during preparation, followed by full scheduler settlement.
- Cancellation after preparation but before publication **through the complete scheduler path**; the service-only version was measured.
- Cross-process cancellation contending with the settlement transaction or immediately after its commit.
- Cancellation combined with lease expiry, owner replacement, token replacement, or lease reacquisition and a stale executor return.
- A complete missing-lease/wrong-owner/wrong-token/expiry-at-boundary matrix at both service preparation and publication.
- Retry or plan revision changes leaving old-attempt output competing with new-attempt output.
- Clock disagreement between scheduler and service expiry checks.
- Process interruption between body preparation, transaction commit, finalization, and discard.
- Cancellation concurrent with result reading/export, or cancellation after previously accepted output committed.

Additional boundaries:

- Exact release revision is unpinned.
- No full schema/trigger audit or underlying transaction-isolation proof was completed.
- No exhaustive publication-caller inventory was supplied.
- No separate latest-attempt/active-plan generation guarantee was established.
- Some R scheduler reads were truncated; its boundary references were recovered through successful line-numbered grep observations. S is the primary scheduler evidence.
- No deployment, live-service, or full-project test evidence was supplied.
- Absence of an explicit check in the inspected methods is not a claim that every storage path and schema constraint lacks it.

For the “cancellation immediately after committed success” case, a future check should first define the intended linearization contract: the cancellation helper leaves already-terminal tasks unchanged. Rejection before commit and treatment after successful commit are different obligations, not one universal late-result rule.

## 8. Recommended release regression checks

These are recommendations, **not tests added or executed during this audit**.

### Priority 0 — establish the central operator-cancellation guarantee

1. **Abort-ignoring executor, successful payload after durable cancellation.**
   - Gate an executor after dispatch.
   - Cancel the task and verify durable cancellation before releasing the gate.
   - Return `{ outcome: "succeeded", results: [...] }`.
   - Assert no accepted or diagnostic publication, no `result-recorded` or `attempt-completed` event, and no task resurrection.
   - Assert cancelled attempt/step settlement with a valid lease; assert appropriate lease-lost accounting when ownership/expiry is no longer valid.
   - Targets the gap at `src/tasks/task-scheduler.test.ts:841–859`.

2. **Full scheduler cancellation between preparation and publication.**
   - Place a deterministic gate after preparation.
   - Cancel before transactional publication.
   - Assert no visible results/completion events and cleanup of uncommitted content.
   - Extends the measured service case at `src/tasks/task-result-service.test.ts:318–371` across the scheduler boundary.

3. **Publication-caller fence contract.**
   - Inventory callers of `record`, `prepare`, and `publishPrepared`.
   - Verify every scheduler-originated settlement carries owner/token and uses the settlement transaction.
   - Add a regression check explicitly defining whether unfenced recording after terminal cancellation is permitted for non-scheduler use. Do not assume universal service rejection under the present optional API.

### Priority 1 — ownership and transactional composition

4. Exercise missing lease, wrong owner, wrong token, expiry equal to current time, elapsed expiry, and cancellation marker at both preparation and publication.
5. Return stale success after lease expiry/reacquisition, and after retry/plan replacement; assert no stale accepted output or task resurrection.
6. Exercise cancellation/settlement contention using separate store connections or processes, with an explicit commit-order expectation.
7. Inject interruption around prepare/commit/finalize/discard and verify metadata/body recovery without deleting committed content.

### Priority 2 — consumer semantics and release reproducibility

8. Preserve deadline diagnostic retention while preventing diagnostic output from becoming accepted primary output or satisfying completion.
9. Define and test retained-output reads/exports after cancellation; verify labeling and acceptance distinctions.
10. Capture the exact release commit and dependency provenance, then rerun the three audited test files and relevant concrete-store/operator suites on that revision. Record final exit codes and counts rather than extrapolating from this audit.

## 9. Final conclusion

The inspected scheduler and result service have a coherent, layered cancellation-fencing protocol for scheduler-originated accepted results. The measured tests support selected cancellation, rollback, and diagnostic-retention behaviors, with **81 observed passes and no observed failures**.

They do **not** establish unconditional late-result rejection across all callers and race orderings. Release assurance should remain explicitly conditional on the scheduler’s fenced transactional publication contract, and any strong claim about operator-cancelled late success should await the targeted end-to-end regression test and verification against a pinned release revision.
